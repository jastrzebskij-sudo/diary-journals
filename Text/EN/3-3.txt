Those to whom this happened say they were asked why the four of them always travel together, and they supposedly answered that in the Polish army one walks in fours, and that in case of arrest a foursome is needed for bridge. The gendarmes believe so firmly that there are always four traveling together that when they catch 2 or 3, they search the whole town until they find the complete foursome.

February 2, 1941

We set out by bus to Marnia, the last town in Algeria on the Moroccan border. There, after leaving town on the road toward Oujda, a police car came upon us and arrested three of our group, namely Fatyga, Grzegorzewski, and Zarychta. I and Jaś Wołk managed to slip away and throw off the pursuit, which was quite numerous and immediate. We at once changed our route from the road to the railway track. We walked along the track all night without being seen by anyone, passing very close by Oujda, as far as the Sidi Bemuki station. Since it was very, very cold with a strong wind, we took shelter in a culvert under the tracks and fell asleep there. We were woken by the whistle of a locomotive.
