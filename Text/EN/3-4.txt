After an hour, two policemen appeared on a motorcycle and arrested us, taking us to Oujda. There they drew up a report and handed us over to the secret police so as not to shame us publicly. There they let us spend the night in the telephone operator's room. On February 4, accompanied by a plainclothes agent, they sent us to Tlemcen. The whole time they treated us very politely, but they would not let us go. In Tlemcen they registered us at the garrison headquarters, and the commander issued us a pass and allowed us to sleep in a hotel, ordering us to report to the railway station the next morning.

In one of the cafés we met a Pole serving in the French army. The next day, accompanied by the plainclothes agent, we traveled to Oran, and there we were joined to those who had met this same fate before us.

We live in a barrack that houses representatives of all three Allies, since there are 4 Englishmen, 3 Frenchmen, and 26 Poles, all held for the same reason. They give us as many passes as we want, and the food is better than in Marseille.

So the situation looks better than in Ste Marthe.
