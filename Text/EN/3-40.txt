June 4, 1941, Wednesday

A great deal is being said about France joining the German side.
All of us find ourselves in spiritual turmoil, not knowing what to
do with ourselves — whether to join the Foreign Legion, or go to the farm as we
call it, to the "paysan," or return to Europe.

June 5, 1941, Thursday

A captain and Lt. Polański arrived from Oran. They went to the head of
agriculture and agreed on the terms of work on the farms: the daily wage is to be
20 francs plus food and lodging. Afterward there was much quarreling at the briefing
with the company because many do not want to go to the farm. Perhaps they have a
point, but someone must go so that we are not all together in one place, because that draws
attention and could harm us.

June 6, 1941, Friday

On the walk in the ravine we found a pepper tree, and now people joke
about us that Hitler banished us to where the pepper grows.

June 7, 1941, Saturday

As usual on Saturdays a bedding inspection was ordered, for which the bedding must be
folded properly, but it did not take place because only a few individuals had done so.
Because of this the command imposed an O.P.R. [drill/punishment exercise]. It must
be admitted, however, that discipline is very good compared to other
camps, which makes this time pass more pleasantly. Besides this, the day was marked by
lively passport activity. They took the radio away for exchange and we do not know
what is happening on the fronts.
