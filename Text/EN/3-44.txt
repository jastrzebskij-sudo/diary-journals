June 18, 1941, Wednesday

Today a German-Italian commission arrived in Saïda for the purpose of inspecting the garrison. But the French drove the legionnaires, us Poles, and the travailleurs out into the field, leaving behind only about 50 unfit men for show. Several of the travailleurs who wanted to reach the commission were locked in the guardhouse. Of the Poles, apparently two, namely Fiszer and Podniesiński, wanted to go speak with the commission, but Maj. Fuzeau did not allow it, saying that those Polish patriots who wish to return to Poland can do so through the German Red Cross and not through the military commission. Everyone did not return to the barracks until 8:30 in the evening. On June 17, 1941, those who had gone to work in the Sahara returned, terribly exhausted.

June 19, 1941, Thursday through June 21, 1941, Saturday

The heat is beginning, reaching up to 50 degrees in the sun at midday. From 12 to 2:30 at midday we have mandatory rest, but it is impossible to sleep from the heat and the flies bite terribly, so we spend this time playing cards.

June 22, 1941, Sunday
An exceptionally happy day.

Returning from church, where our choir sang beautifully and the church was full of people, we learned that the Germans had attacked Russia and that the English had taken Damascus. Unusual joy prevailed, because whatever the outcome of this encounter, instead of one enemy we shall have fewer. Two hours later I received a letter from my closest family back home saying that they are well and living in their own house. This is the first letter from home to me. The occasion should have been toasted, but there was nothing to toast with; however, in the evening Dziadkowiec turned up wanting to play cards, we agreed to play for beer, and again luck was with me, for I won 4 beers and we immediately toasted the occasion.

June 23, 1941, Monday

In the afternoon while bathing in the pool I struck my knee, which swelled up immediately. Bathing in such heat is a great pleasure.

June 24, 1941, Tuesday

In the afternoon our doctor, Przedborski (from Radom), gave a talk on the topic of venereal diseases. The matter presents itself terribly among us: out of a strength of 200, 36 are sick with gonorrhea and 12 with syphilis, and a further 6 are under observation, suspected of this latter disease. The doctor implored us to be more restrained, describing what lies ahead. The garrison command has also taken up prevention of these diseases; to this end they have ordered that special written permits be obtained from the doctor in order to be able to use the brothel, where one of the NCOs is on duty, not admitting anyone without these permits, and collecting them, and a medical orderly who performs the procedures.

Furthermore, the garrison commander announced that anyone who falls ill will be punished. Such is the state of affairs after a four-month stay, and what will come next?
