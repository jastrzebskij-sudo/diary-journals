We had a wonderful time, singing the Frenchmen Polish songs and they singing us French ones; we raised toasts to France, and they did the same for us in return.

We parted as warm friends. During the farewell, Sr. Pvt. Strękowski vanished from our sight. We searched for him for about half an hour without success.

So we gave up the further search and set off on the way back. Along the way I began to feel unwell and had to ride to ......

and afterward by bicycle, so as to get home sooner, because I was very weak. The bicycle belonged to the French corporal, but he hadn't wanted to ride it while walking with us, and he handed it to me when I grew weak.

I got back to my billet at 3 o'clock in the morning.

Monday, March 25, 1940

On the second day of the holiday we don't go to church, and I spend the whole day lying in bed — pardon, on the straw — resting. Janowski and I tell each other about how our trip went, and we poke fun at Sgt. Dziadkowiec and Cpl. Dzierzyk, and they at us. Cpl. Dzierzyk and Sgt. Dziadkowiec spent the holiday in Avignon.

Tuesday, March 26, 1940. Exercises with the tank.

Wednesday, March 27, 1940. It's been raining since morning, and lectures all day at billet 46.
