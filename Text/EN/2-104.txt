December 30 and 31, Monday and Tuesday
Terrible boredom. Truly, such idleness under these conditions could
drive a man to madness. Thoughts of the past, of family, of Poland keep
coming to mind, and worst of all are thoughts of the future.

January 1, 1941, New Year
The French somehow celebrate this day more festively than Christmas.
Even the dinner was a genuine New Year's dinner. I, however, spend the
whole day in the barracks. Only on New Year's Eve did I spend time at
the cinema.

January 2 and 3, 1941
No changes at all. In these days especially, cold and hunger plague us,
because they feed us nothing but weeds. It reminds me of the stories old
peasants used to tell, about how in years of famine they ate goosefoot.
We eat the very same thing today. We hold no grudge against the
French, though, since they give the same to their own troops. But in
Ste Marthe, where the Poles are, the cooking is worlds better.

January 4, 1941
A radio bulletin reports that Australian units have captured the Italian
fortress of "Bardia" in Libya, where they took 25 thousand prisoners,
including three generals.
