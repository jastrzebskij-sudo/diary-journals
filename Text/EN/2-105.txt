January 5, 1941
From our friends in La Penme we receive a letter with New Year's
wishes, along with a letter they had received from Roman Długosz in
Algiers. He apparently fled to England and the French caught him in
Africa; he's sitting in prison. He's a decent fellow, only he's advancing
rather too quickly, since he's already a sergeant. I left a suitcase with
my things in the barracks in Ste Marthe, since I don't need it here, and
there they stole from me a shirt I had bought in Narbonne. So I took the
suitcase to Marseille.

January 6, 1941. Epiphany
Nothing worth noting.

January 7, 1941.
In the evening I got a little tipsy on wine, so much so that the next day I
was ill, since it doesn't take much for me.
Until January 10 we do nothing. News reached us that the Polish priest
(the one who got beaten up by Fiedosiejew) has returned from Spain, in
order to show the way through Spain.
January 10, 1941
As a result of an agreement among the French command, our quarters
are taken from us, and we are forced to return to Ste Marthe.
