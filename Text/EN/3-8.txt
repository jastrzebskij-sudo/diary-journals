In the evening we were not allowed to leave the barracks despite having passes. The reason was probably that the English fleet was cruising near the port, and indeed several lights could be seen out at sea.

February 21, 1941, Friday

After dinner we go to the station in fours and load onto the train; two carriages are specially reserved, one second class and the other third class, and we travel to Saïda, about 200 km south of Oran. We traveled all night.

February 22, 1941, Saturday

At 7 o'clock in the morning we get off at Saïda, eat breakfast in town, and go to the barracks. We arrive just as the flag is being raised. The garrison commander, Major Fiseau, received us very politely. The barracks are fairly nice, sleeping is comfortable, and the food is very good. Strict rules apply here — one may go into town only between 6 and 9 in the evening. The town itself is very small but clean. The temperature is much lower than in Oran, since we are at an altitude of about 1000 m above sea level. At night, even under three blankets and a sheet, one could freeze.
First order. We are named the First African Company. Discipline is announced.
