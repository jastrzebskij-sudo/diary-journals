April 6, 1941, Saturday
Today I went to confession. It was a general confession, since almost no one knows French. This was my first confession of this kind. I found it a bit strange, and somehow it seemed to me that it wasn't valid, but later I made my peace with it.
The company commander, Lt. Piątkowski, again punished the whole company with barracks confinement because apparently two men were late and gave false names at the guardhouse.

April 7, 1941, Palm Sunday
The company goes up to Holy Communion, and with us a great many Frenchwomen. There are very many French people at our Holy Mass. The camp was visited by some major (Wysoczański), apparently the senior Polish officer in North Africa. He held a briefing with us in which he noted that we might yet visit many more countries. In the afternoon, a football match against the team we played last Sunday, this time losing 2–1. Our team's play was beneath criticism; half the audience left already at halftime.
The radio news reports:
The Germans have attacked Yugoslavia and Greece.
General Sikorski signed an agreement in Canada on forming a Polish army in Canada and left for Washington. The English took Addis Ababa in Abyssinia.
