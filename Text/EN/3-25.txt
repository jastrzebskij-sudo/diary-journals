April 17, 1941, Thursday

The weather improved considerably, so that we could go for a walk. News from the Balkans and from Libya is very bad. The Germans have already entered Egyptian territory. The soap ration cards, which the town hall had issued to us by mistake, were taken back from us.

April 18, 1941, Friday

This evening was marked by a triumph of Polish song, sung by the company choir at a concert whose proceeds were earmarked for French national defense. The concert was held in the hall of the Pallace cinema and drew a large crowd of guests; the hall was packed. This concert, dull at first, was livened up only by our choir. The mere announcement of it already drew a storm of applause.

And when one of our comrades first explained in French the content of the song the choir was about to sing, such silence fell over the hall that you could have heard a fly go by, so intently did they listen to him. It should be noted here that the audience had behaved rather noisily during the earlier parts of the program. The curtain rose, and the first song to flow out was "Rozwiń swe skrzydła białe" (Unfurl your white wings). The beautiful melody, along with the conductor's movements, which made an impression both to the ear and to the eye, as though he were playing some great and mighty instrument, so captivated the audience that when they finished, the applause rang out with redoubled force.
