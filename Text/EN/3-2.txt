Africa

January 20, 1941. Oran

In the evening the sailors took us into the city disguised as sailors, providing us with their seaman's books and bluffing their way past the policemen with loud talk. They led us to an agreed café, where gentlemen who deal with these matters were already waiting for us. They already knew that we were coming and how much money we had. They told us to wait in Oran for a while, showing us where a cheap hotel was and where a cheap restaurant was. The owner of this restaurant, who is also the waiter, is a former Spanish consul. The food is fairly tasty and clean. The hotel where we are staying is very dirty, and its owner is an Arab. One day he brought us big 16-year-old Arab girls for entertainment. They are very passionate. After a week we changed hotels, because Wicek Zarychta, brother of Sergeant Zarychta of the castle gendarmerie, found lice. In the second hotel we took 3 rooms, because we were counting on Kurniak joining us, who had arrived with the next group. But he was not let out, and I live alone in that room, while the five of us pay our share scrupulously.

Africa, or rather Oran, made a very pleasant impression on us. It is a spotlessly clean, modern city; only the sight of Arabs in national dress reminds one that one is in Africa. The tap water here is very salty, probably because it is drawn from the sea.
