October 6, 1940, Sunday
A battalion of French troops, surrounded on all sides by gendarmes, arrived
at the camp here. They are probably soldiers returning from Africa. They were
locked up in several barracks surrounded by barbed wire and are closely
guarded by double sentries of both soldiers and gendarmerie. They are
undergoing quarantine here.
The whole day I did not go anywhere.

October 7, 1940, Monday
We march to work singing, as indeed we did before too.
The French like it, because they come out of their barracks and listen with great
interest. I have not yet heard the French army sing.
And once more we hand over photographs, two apiece. I must have already
given out about fifteen photographs, with no result.
Today at noon I lost my watch in the washroom.

October 8, 1940, Tuesday
For two days now the Pole Edward (a cadet officer) has been promising us that
there will be a radio, and there isn't one. The company gives him no peace over it.
