We receive pay totaling 722 francs, and after deducting 140 francs for food for a period of two weeks, 582 francs remained.

After lunch I went into town with Janek Sadecki, but nothing could be bought because the meager supplies that were there had been bought up by Spaniards who are returning to Spain from the internment camps. In the evening at a café I met Fredek Rydlewski with two Englishmen. One of them had run off after quarreling drunkenly with his companions, having taken Fredek's revolver. I treated them to an omelet and beer so as to repay the Englishmen for what they had given me when I was on leave. They last received their pay in May, so they are hard up.

August 8, Thursday

During the morning session Lt. Osiecki made us younger or older, for the purpose of changing our passports so we could leave. Three new photographs are needed for a new passport.

After lunch I played volleyball with the Englishmen, but they don't know how to play. In the evening I was at the café with Janek. We only got potatoes in sauce, and wine, of which there is plenty. For a few days now they've been selling grapes, costing 3.50 cnt.

August 9, Friday

Our platoon is on kitchen duty, and during this work Kanar took a photo of us. I wrote a letter to Irenka.

In the evening I was at the radio at Mr. Cymerman's.
