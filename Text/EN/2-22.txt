Sunday, February 25, 1940

After the service, at which the parish priest (a Frenchman who doesn't know Polish) intoned "Boże coś Polskę," I lost 6 francs playing cards with Sgt. Gbur. At 2 p.m. we go on a trip into the mountains, with a French corporal named Roman and Cpl. Świątkowski Tadeusz. We walked over three hours one way. In the town of Gigondes we stopped to buy wine. When the restaurant owner learned we were Poles, he wouldn't take payment from us for the 2 liters of wine. On top of the rocks we built a cairn of stones with a note bearing a suitable inscription, tucked inside my old worn coin purse. Then we sang the national anthem and headed back down. Along the way we stopped once more at the little café mentioned above, and ate a supper consisting of an omelet, 2 liters of wine, roasted potatoes, cheese, and stewed pears. In the place there were also 3 officers of the French air force and 6 privates of the same unit. The restaurant owner's daughters, very pretty and pleasant girls, took such good care of us that I count that evening among the nicest of my life. I returned to Ste Cecile at 24:30 (half past midnight).
