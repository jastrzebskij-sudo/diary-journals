In the morning the nearby residents were already giving us breakfast; my companion in misfortune received 25 kilograms of grapes from some gentleman. We had food beyond counting, and everyone walked around pleased at how warmly we were received. Mr. Podsudek, a Pole who owned a bacon factory, made the greatest effort on our behalf. Around noon the police drove us out beyond the city so as not to obstruct traffic, which we managed only with great difficulty. We stood outside the city on the highway for 2 days and 2 nights; everyone sold whatever they could, and I did the same, earning 2,000 lei that way. We could not travel further because we lacked fuel. The Romanians would not give us any and instead ordered us to hand over our vehicles at the Makabi sports field. We did not want to do this, but I received an order from Major Segda and drove my van to the sports stadium. The Romanians loaded all the drivers onto trucks and drove them off to a camp; I had already decided to go along and put my knapsack onto the truck. The previous night someone had stolen my suitcase containing a large number of photographs.
