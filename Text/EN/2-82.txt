said that he firmly believed Poland and France would rise again.
The audience answered with a shout of "Vive la France," the colonel
added "Vive la Pologne," and to him, in Polish, three times "niech żyje" [long live].
After that bonfire I felt greatly uplifted in spirit.
Our French friends came to visit us, but I did not see them. Janek S. and Józek R.
saw them off. They are said to have been quite offended with me over that. The radio
reports that the Italian offensive on Egypt has been halted and that General de Gaulle
has landed with troops at Dakar.

September 24, 1940, Tuesday
I went with Misiek W. to the highest mountain peak in this area. From
there Marseille could be seen as if on the palm of one's hand. Misiek was going to take
photographs, but poor visibility got in our way. We promised each other that as
soon as the weather is suitable we will go there again to take pictures.
Misiek climbed the rocks in such a way that it even amazed me. Janek and Józek
brought back photographs from Cassis. They came out well. The situation at Dakar with General de
Gaulle is unclear.
