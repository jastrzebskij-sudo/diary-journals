Today Lt. Piątkowski, commander of our company, carried out a verification of the aspirants; officially we have no news of it, but rumors are going around that he thoroughly weeded them out — apparently there are only 5 left instead of 24. The NCOs are also said to be checked. And a good thing too, since 80 percent had promoted themselves. I won't name names here, since such dirty laundry is better kept quiet, but you could count on your fingers those who actually hold their ranks properly.

March 28, Friday
They are preparing a record of each of us. An officer arrived from Oran for this purpose. I am helping with the writing.

March 29, 1941, Saturday
Finishing the records. This week I resigned from the choir because of constant quarrels.

March 30, 1941, Sunday
As every Sunday, a service, and after dinner our sports team plays a friendly match against Saïda's reserve team and wins 3–2, one goal for Saïda being an own goal. Two Frenchmen bet each other 550 francs on the outcome of this match, and so our players each got 50 francs.

March 31, 1941, Monday
Our football team receives kits from the Red Cross in Algiers, and besides that there is a net and a ball for volleyball.
