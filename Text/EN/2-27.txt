At 10 o'clock, the święcone at the great gateway of billet 23. The company commander, Capt. Kruczyński, was not present, being away on a course. Lt. Skowroński was on leave, and Lt. Osiecki stood in for them.

Breakfast was good and plentiful, beautifully laid out and decorated with flowers, which our French neighbors gladly supplied us.

Everything waits for the Major. At last he arrives, accompanied by his chief of staff, receives the report, and offers us his wishes in these words:

"I will not wish you a Happy Easter, because it cannot be happy so far from our families, who are suffering back there in our distant Homeland — but I do wish you the swift receipt of modern armored equipment, and a return home with it as victors."

Then Maj. Habowski shared an egg with Lt. Osiecki and the delegates of the NCOs and privates, sat at the table for a few minutes, and went on to the other companies.

The various companies sent us their own delegations with good wishes, and we sent them the same. The święcone ended in good spirits, with singing. Everyone had a good drink, while our dear sergeant-major just went around encouraging everyone to sing the song "Czas do domu, Czas" ("Time to Go Home, Time").

After we broke up, the four of us — myself, Janowski, Chaniewski, and Stękowski — set off on a trip into the mountains, to the place I had already been to once, namely Gigadas.

I went up to the summit where I had left the coin purse with the note bearing our names the last time.

After coming down from the mountains we stopped at the little café we knew, and there I met Cpl.
