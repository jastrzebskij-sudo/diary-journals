On returning to the vehicles I found a second lieutenant of the Air Force waiting for me, as the senior man of this small group of vehicles, who proposed forming a sabotage unit. Of course I agreed very willingly. The second lieutenant told us to drive to the border crossing point to draw fuel, while he himself went to look for more. At the border point there were no longer any vehicles with fuel, and the second lieutenant, after talking with Major Segda, gave up on the sabotage unit idea. We stood at the border in a field the whole day, not knowing what to do with ourselves. The odometer on my vehicle read 13,000.

It is Irena's name day, the name day of my last sweetheart from Warsaw. Before evening the decision was made to cross the border. I removed the eagle insignia from my shoulder straps and cap, put on a beret instead, and off we went. Crossing the border I felt so pained that I could not hold back tears; I glanced sideways at my traveling companion, whose surname I don't remember, and saw that he too was crying, so I could no longer restrain myself and wept aloud.
