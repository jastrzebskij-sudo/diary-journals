September 16, 1940, Monday
At lunchtime we receive notice that our company is to be
demobilized in the afternoon. For this purpose an assembly was held to
discuss what to do. Some wanted to sign, others did not; after long arguments
it was settled that everyone should do as he liked. And so the company went to be demobilized,
because apparently some major (which one I don't know) said that we would
receive the 1000 fr. demobilization bonus before long. As we were going to the
demobilization, I ran into Janek Sadecki near our French
friends, who had come by on a stroll to visit us. I brought Magi and
Monika into the barrack so they could call for Rej and
Wolski. This caused such merriment that the whole barrack came running to see them.
They took my little tank from me, and only at their mother's intervention did they give it back to me. 2nd Lt.
Chromiński lent me 50 fr. as soon as I gave him to understand what I needed, and
we went to the café in the valley, where we had some beer, ate some fruit,
and chatted a little — partly in words and partly by hand. Afterward we
walked the ladies and children home, while the husband of the plumper one rode
ahead on a bicycle. Along the way Wolski took a few photos of us, and the time
passed very merrily. Once there, we ate supper for that 50 fr., consisting
of a ham omelet and 1 liter of wine.
