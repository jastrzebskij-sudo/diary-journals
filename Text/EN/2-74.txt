September 4, 1940, Wednesday
Before evening I went with Józek Rej and Janek Sadecki to
the small town of Penne, to that French family already mentioned above,
where we used to go for the radio. This couple invited us for figs, which grow here
half-wild. For the first time in my life I ate figs not dried but
straight from the tree. They resemble small pears and taste
very sweet and are very sticky. We also ate a few sweet
almonds, picking them straight off the tree. They are similar to apricots, and one eats
the pit. The shell splits like on a chestnut, and that pit
remains on the tree. Next we had them make us a salad of 1 kg of tomatoes
with the addition of cucumbers, onion, and vinegar.
We ate our fill properly, paying only 3 fr. 50 cm. The line that supplies
the electricity for lighting this house had been damaged and we couldn't
listen to the radio, but in this situation the couple's brother-in-law came to our rescue and
invited us to his place, as he lives nearby.
The news broadcast from London at 9:15 p.m. began with a speech by
the President of the Republic of Poland, Raczkiewicz. Then came bombardments
on both sides, and the handing over by the United States of 50
destroyers. Roman Długosz was also with us at the broadcast, along with a friend.
