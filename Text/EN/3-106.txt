The political situation is such that it's not clear whether it would be better to escape to the English from Africa or from Europe. As he left, the major took Tomaszewski with him under arrest, and also took Chief Benal to the colonel in Colomb-Béchar to discuss our case. After the major left, the company took food, and those who had most urged the hunger strike now sit like frightened rabbits.

January 26, 1942, Monday
In today's orders it was announced that as long as work output remains at 1 cubic meter per person, we will not get wine and the canteen will not be opened. The company commander punished Tomaszewski with 8 days' solitary confinement, separated from the group. But despite such a strict order, the output we achieve today is 1/3 of what's required, because the stone we haul is available only in very small quantities, at a distance of 300 m from the track. I have the impression that they won't be opening the canteen — where I can buy dates, which I'm mad about — anytime soon.

January 27, 1942, Tuesday
Output is the same as yesterday, but at the request of the Polish and French overseers, the commander allowed wine to be issued and the canteen to open. That very evening I spent over 50 francs on purchases. Tonight Chief Benal arrived back from Colomb-Béchar, where he had been summoned to the Second Bureau rather than to the colonel, as he had been told. There, apparently, he got a proper dressing-down for stirring up the company. On the same train, Sierota and Ołota departed for Colomb-Béchar.

January 28, 1942, Wednesday
Today a new commander arrived at the camp, in the rank of captain — an older man, very fierce-looking, with a bristling mustache. He limps slightly, and on his chest are many decorations. On the night train, 3 Poles arrived: Piekarski, who once escaped from Saïda and was subsequently imprisoned, and two others who claimed to be officers, but those who came to our company from Morocco know them and say they are only privates.

January 29, 1942, Thursday
The new company commander is partly taking over the company.

January 30, 1942, Friday
January 31, 1942, Saturday
Today's train brought us 30 sheep. All these sheep escaped during the night, and when the comrade who tends the flock tried to bring them back, the Arabs turned him back at gunpoint. In the morning the commander sent the Arabs out searching and threatened that if the sheep weren't found, they would pay for it. Around 10 o'clock an Arab brought back one, and by noon they had brought back 28 head, along with one torn hide. The Arabs claim jackals devoured the sheep. On examining the hide, however, it turned out the skin had been cut with a knife.
