One day Lieutenant Włodzimierz Gulin arrived and stayed temporarily in my quarters. About 3 weeks before Lieutenant Gulin, Mr. Baranowski had arrived and is in the 7th Officer Cadet Company.

The officer cadet companies moved to quarters at Guer, and the 11th Company to Comblessac. I tried to get a transfer to the Armored Corps or to one of the infantry regiments, but Lieutenant Colonel Hofbauer would not allow it. The Armored Battalion is commanded by Captain Ropelewski, former commander of the President's Motor Column. Lieutenant Jaroński is also there. Around December 10, 1939, a transport of 500 men arrived from Romania and Hungary. Among them are Warrant Officers Zygmunt Czajkowski and Sępkowski. On the next transport Corporal Świtajewski arrived from the Castle Column.

From the last class year of the Castle Company only Senior Rifleman Czesław Grześkowiak arrived, and he went off to the 4th Infantry Regiment.

The assembly station grew by a few more barracks and the staff also grew, to the point of excess, so that I now do virtually nothing.

For a time Lieutenant Colonel Hofbauer was replaced by Cadet Officer Strutyński, later by Cavalry Captain Rudnicki, who fell ill from the work, and in his place came Cavalry Captain Kalinowski. Matters concerning non-commissioned officers are handled by Second Lieutenant Łowczowski and Warrant Officer Czapiński. One of the assistants is Corporal Raczkowski. The head of the Station's Supply Section is Sergeant Leon Winogrodzki.

With this I end the summary and begin writing daily entries.

/signed/ Stefan Pisulewski, Corporal
