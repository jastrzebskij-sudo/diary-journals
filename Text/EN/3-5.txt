Capt. Iwanowski, who fled to England. We feel great shame, because the whole town is talking about it.

In the evening I go to the cinema to see a film that a French studio produced in honor of the Polish heroine Emilia Plater. A fairly good film, though not strictly accurate historically.

February 9, 1941, Saturday, and February 10, Sunday

I play cards; although I won only 30 francs, everyone looks at me askance.

One of the Arabs got into a drunken fight with an Englishman; the Poles helped the Englishman, but the next day the Arab came and apologized to the Englishman and the Poles.

February 10, Monday

Lt. Polański brings us news that the English terribly bombed Genoa in Italy.

While in town I bought myself a chess set; they are very hard to find, because the French are a very lazy nation and don't feel like playing a game where one has to think a little. On my way back from town I witnessed a scene the Germans could be proud of. Namely, two imprisoned Germans, who had been traveling to England for sabotage work by the same route as us, were being transported somewhere by car, guarded by 8 armed soldiers and two French NCOs — the Germans were therefore in chains. And the French soldiers looked as if they were very afraid, while the Germans laughed scornfully.
