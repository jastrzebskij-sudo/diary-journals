July 10, Wednesday
We received our pay, a total of 829 fr. After lunch I go into town wanting to buy a shirt. But I couldn't get one; I only bought slippers for 12 francs.
Corporal Wojtkowiak, who had stayed behind at the positions with Platoon Sergeant Sum, joined the company and told us about the death of Platoon Sergeant Sum and his driver. According to his account, the tank was knocked out by the French with a 75mm gun, by mistake. The sergeant did not die inside the tank but had left it because it was on fire. The shell tore off his head and both arms, and his entrails spilled out.
Driver Senior Private Świecicki was severely wounded in the left leg near the hip and in the left arm in two places, and also had several shrapnel fragments in his stomach. Doctors from the French Red Cross took him to the hospital, but held out no hope of keeping him alive.

July 11, Thursday
Boredom, an almost hopeless wait — several ships have already sailed from the port, and no one wants to talk with us. Our passports have been made, but there are no visas. Corporal Jodłowski took his passport without a visa from the lieutenant and is trying to arrange things on his own.
It's raining, and sitting idly in the barrack like this, I ponder our situation, and despair overtakes us — we are almost without a way out.
