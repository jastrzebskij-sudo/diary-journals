January 23, 1942, Friday
Seeing our slow work, the camp commander ordered the chief to assemble the company; he himself alerted the Arabs who still guard us, and only then, revolver in hand, began speaking sharply to us, announcing that if the company did not work at the rate of 1 cubic meter per person every day, he would not allow wine and meat to be issued, and would close the canteen.
By noon the pace of work had not improved, so there was no wine at lunch, and the camp commander began provoking some kind of incident. After lunch the company again worked slowly. A few minutes before the end of work the camp commander came over and began accosting several men for working too slowly, but they gave way to him — until he ordered Tomaszewski to light a fire and get to the wheelbarrows; the latter did not carry out the order, for which the camp commander personally marched him off to the arrest area. In the company someone shouted, "Don't let this happen, let's drop our tools!" and the whole company left its tools and started walking toward the camp, but the Austrian blocked their path and asked them to gather up the tools for him. The company turned back and gathered the tools.
While this was happening, the camp commander summoned the Arabs and blocked the company's path, but the company paid no attention to this and walked past unhindered. Only once the company had dispersed to the tents did he post guards by the tents, one Arab per two tents, and forbade anyone to leave them under threat of being shot at. When supper was announced, no one came out for it, and so the hunger strike began. We feel this was not the right course to take, but it's already done.

January 24, 1941, Saturday
We go hungry, lying in the tents. The weather is foul, as the sirocco blows with great force, so we wouldn't have gone to work anyway. In the company, the cooks and those on duty, like the storekeeper and the orderly, do not go hungry, because the latter is very afraid. The cooks secretly brought us a small piece of bread with onion at noon, and a cup of soup for supper. They did this so openly with the soup that it seems the French must have noticed.

January 25, 1942, Sunday
During the night more Arabs arrived by truck, and around 10 in the morning the major who commands the travailleurs arrived from Colomb-Béchar. He summoned Chief Benal and spoke with him. After this conversation the chief assembled the company and announced that the major had said he could not improve the food, and rejected our other demands as well; he only promised that we would get the money we were asking for, for which he gave his word of honor, and that if he could find another water tank somewhere, we would have Saturday afternoons free for laundry. As for Tomaszewski, he said that the man had disobeyed an order and would be court-martialed for it. As for the company, if it wants to starve or work, that's up to it — he would not force us. Here he reminded us that one condition of the armistice was that Poles be handed over to the Germans, but that they [the French] are hiding us — yet if we cause trouble, the German Commission will find out about us and take us away. Hearing this, Sgt. Gbur immediately ran to the major asking to be sent to Germany, adding that there were plenty of volunteers here for that.
