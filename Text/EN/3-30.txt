April 29, 1941, Tuesday

Lt. Piątkowski held a company briefing on the subject of the Red Cross delegate's visit. We were promised a great deal in kind, and daily income, together with pay, is to amount to 4 francs a day, with payment to be made weekly. He also drew attention to the spreading tall tales, referring mainly to the matter of Mr. Rzepecki from Oran. I found in "Wiarus Polski" (The Polish Veteran) some words of wisdom applicable to this matter, so I am pasting them in as a keepsake. Many comrades admit that during our military service in France we did not have it as good as here.

Newspaper clipping:
A good man draws moral benefit even from evil; a wicked man finds fault even in what is good. L.

April 30, 1941, Wednesday, Foreign Legion Day

It is a day observed very solemnly, much like regimental holidays in Poland. It is celebrated in memory of the battle in Mexico. Wake-up was half an hour earlier, followed by the solemn raising of the standard, a reading by the garrison commander of an account of that battle, the decoration of distinguished legionnaires, then a parade in which the Poles also took part, and, at the garrison commander's request, singing after the parade. After the parade, a service at which our choir sang, and immediately afterward the laying of wreaths at the legionnaires' monument. The Poles also laid a wreath, and the choir sang the Marseillaise.
