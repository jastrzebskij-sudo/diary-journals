A few stations before Dijon we turn east and travel all day. There, the whole transport nearly fell into German hands. Thanks to Captain Ziemski's cunning, we escape back. At a certain station not far from Dijon our company (i.e., first Company, second Battalion) plunders a freight car of chocolate and pickles. We unload at Dijon. A French general arrived and gave us orders for the defense of that city. They supplied us with fuel, but the captain got out of it and we drive at night toward Roanne. There we were again put to work to defend the city along a thirty-kilometers stretch, first Company. We took up positions defending the city. We stood there the whole night and day. During the day Italian planes bombed us. In the evening the captain gave the order to withdraw, and as it later turned out, without the French's orders. We were to go by platoons to the town of Le Puy. In the town of Toy the French authorities arrested us, saying that Captain Ziemski was under arrest; Lieutenant Osiecki was also arrested. Finally an understanding was reached and Lieutenant Osiecki was released, but we know nothing about the captain. The French ordered us to defend the small town of Ste Just, near Ste Etienne. Here news reached us that we are handing over the equipment to the French and the Polish army is going to England. Part of the tanks we hand over at that location, and six go to Monistrol, and there is a misunderstanding there, because the French captain thought that we didn't want to give up the tanks but were fleeing with them to England. Finally an understanding was reached and they gave us food. We drive to Le Puy by trucks, the whole unit led by Lieutenant Stanek. There the French gave us orders to go to Marseille.
