June twenty-fifth, nineteen forty, Tuesday We arrive at Port Vendres. We find three Polish soldiers whose ship has already left. They tell us that five hours before our arrival an English ship departed which took one,nine hundred Poles from the first Division.

June twenty-fifth, nineteen forty, Wednesday While waiting for a ship I tour the nearby surroundings of the port. The port is situated in the mountains, almost natural. Guarded by coastal artillery. In the port stand three French destroyers and three French merchant ships. While touring the beautiful rocky coast I bathe in the sea for the first time. The water is salty and soap won't lather at all, so you can't do laundry. In the evening a Yugoslav freighter, the Petar, puts into port. The company waits for a ship on the harbor square, joined by stragglers from other Polish units and Polish civilians. two of them are even married with their wives. Our officers are paying a lot of attention to one of them, a young and pretty one. About ten French tank crewmen join our company, wanting to keep fighting, but the French government won't allow them to leave. They are now learning Polish. The thought inevitably comes to mind: "what goes around comes around" (lit. "the scythe's turn has come for Maciek too"). We used to learn French, and now they [learn] Polish.
