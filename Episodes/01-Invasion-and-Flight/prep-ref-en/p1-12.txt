A few days later several riflemen arrived from the motor column, and Corporal of police, formerly a rifleman of the Castle Company, Dziadek. One day Lieutenant Wlodzimierz Gulin arrived and stayed temporarily in my quarters. About three weeks before Lieutenant Gulin, Mister Baranowski had arrived and is in the seventh Officer Cadet Company.

The officer cadet companies moved to quarters at Guer, and the eleventh Company to Comblessac. I tried to get a transfer to the Armored Corps or to one of the infantry regiments, but Lieutenant Colonel Hofbauer would not allow it. The Armored Battalion is commanded by Captain Ropelewski, former commander of the President's Motor Column. Lieutenant Yaronski is also there. Around December tenth, nineteen thirty-nine, a transport of five hundred men arrived from Romania and Hungary. Among them are Warrant Officers Zygmunt Czajkowski and Sepkowski. On the next transport Corporal Świtajewski arrived from the Castle Column.

From the last class year of the Castle Company only Senior Rifleman Czeslaw Grzeskowiak arrived, and he went off to the fourth Infantry Regiment.

The assembly station grew by a few more barracks and the staff also grew, to the point of excess, so that I now do virtually nothing.

For a time Lieutenant Colonel Hofbauer was replaced by Cadet Officer Strutynski, later by Cavalry Captain Rudnicki, who fell ill from the work, and in his place came Cavalry Captain Kalinowski. Matters concerning non-commissioned officers are handled by Second Lieutenant Łowczowski and Warrant Officer Czapinski. One of the assistants is Corporal Raczkowski. The head of the Station's Supply Section is Sergeant Leon Winogrodzki.

With this I end the summary and begin writing daily entries.

/signed/ Stefan Pisulewski, Corporal Chronicle
