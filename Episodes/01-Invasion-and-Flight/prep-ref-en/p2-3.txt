December fifteenth, nineteen thirty-nine. Warrant Officer Czapinski caught the first louse. I enrolled in the drama circle and was given the role of Kacper, one of the Three Kings. Walczak got hold of a bottle of delicious wine from somewhere.

December sixteenth. A transport arrives from Belgium (about one hundred fifty men); we welcome them with a band. I receive an invitation to spend the holidays in Lille from a certain Mister Stanislaw Budynek, whom I happened to meet in a café and, while drunk, gave him my address. I don't know what to do.

At the drama circle meeting my role was changed to Balthazar.

I wrote back to Mister Budynek that I would not be coming for the holidays. We arranged ourselves a magnificent bath in little tubs used for carrying coal. Second Lieutenant Łowczowski lent me fifty francs.

December seventeenth, Sunday

I got up in good spirits, singing. I was at High Mass in church. The band played and the priest gave an uplifting sermon. After the service the band gave a concert on the square. In the afternoon Golebiowski and Szpak visited me, not counting the regular guests, Corporal Pawlowski and Corporal Matyjasik. There was also another corporal whose surname I don't know, a sailor, a solid fellow. We had food and drink beyond counting. The frost was quite severe and our coal ration was greatly reduced.

December eighteenth, Monday

A transport of about ten hundred men arrived. I took charge of three barracks in the H.eighty-one area, seventy-nine, seventy-seven.
