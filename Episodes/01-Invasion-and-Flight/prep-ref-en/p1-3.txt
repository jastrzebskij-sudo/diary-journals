After the planes flew off, a few more vehicles joined us. Among those who arrived was Sergeant Vrona, head of the garage on Goorchevska Street. Around one in the afternoon we set off again, one by one so as to be safer from air raids, with the assembly point designated at the barracks of the Armored Battalion in Lublin.

I arrived first; after some time a few more vehicles joined. By eight in the evening none of the officers had arrived, and we had no fuel. After a conference we drove onto the highway toward Hewm. We spent the night on the highway. Toward morning the Park Column of the second Battalion passed by, under the command of Captain Ciaglinski, and they gave us fuel; I joined this column because I saw order here. Traffic on the highway was so heavy it was hard to drive in a column. In Hewm we learned that a motor column was standing in the forest beyond the town, so we drove to join it. Just as we reached it, German planes swept in and bombed the railway stations where our column had stood five minutes earlier. We drove on toward Wootsk; nothing important happened along the way. In Wootsk the column took different streets, and the rest of us, through one driver's carelessness, went in a different direction. We lost a couple of hours finding the column.
