That was one of the worst nights. We drove through Brody to Podkamien.

At Podkamien we stood in the forest for one day, and with nightfall we drove on through Tarnopol, Trembowla, Kopyczynce. We stopped in the forest near Czortkow. Around eight o'clock planes flew over, but from the east, which greatly puzzled me, and they circled over the forest for a long time. Two hours later one of the officers rushed in and ordered engines started; in great haste we abandoned the camp and drove by side roads to Czortkow. It was Sunday and there was unusual activity in town. A few kilometers past Czortkow we met a column of Soviet tanks. We passed them. They behaved very politely; we offered them cigarettes and they said they were coming to help us, that they were coming to beat the Germans. We reached Horodenka, where we witnessed a bombing.

From there to quarters at Serafince. We spent the night in Serafince and I repaired the clutch. In the morning I went out to gather news, and it turned out the column had crossed the Romanian border during the night. I met Major Segda, who had also stayed behind at the quarters, and the column had left without waking him.
