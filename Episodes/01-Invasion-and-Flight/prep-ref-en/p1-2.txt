I begin this diary with a summary of past events, starting from the moment war broke out, that is, from September first, nineteen thirty-nine. On that historic day, being at Fort Vola for supplies, I was bombed; several bombs fell very close by. After the air raid, which took place around three in the afternoon, many people still did not believe it was war. The next few days passed under the threat of continual bombardment.

On September sixth I left Warsaw, evacuating the remaining equipment of the Supply Company of the third Armored Battalion. The column, under the command of Lieutenant Yaronski, waited for the rest of the Battalion at Saska Kempa. Around eleven o'clock the rest arrived and we set off for Lublin via Otvotsk. We drove without lights; fortunately the night was moonlit. The column drove in complete order along the Vistula embankment, but once it reached the Warsaw-Lublin highway, where there was enormous congestion, the column fell apart. Near Garvolin I met several vehicles from our column, but none of our officers were among them. We stood for an hour, then drove on; past Riki we were attacked by German aircraft and took cover in the forest.
