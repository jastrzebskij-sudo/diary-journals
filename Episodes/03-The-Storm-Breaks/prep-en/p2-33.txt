Along the way there were a few funny scenes, brought on by my not knowing French. I got off the bus and tried to communicate with the French, but without success, so I went into a shop over which hung a sign bearing a Polish surname. And I chose well, for I met two Poles there, and they took me to Mister and Mrs. Ciemniak's. I recognized Irenka at once, since I had seen her in a photograph. They received us very warmly, and we went to sleep, as it was late.

Sunday, May fifth, nineteen forty , first day of leave At eight in the morning I go with Irenka to Polish Holy Mass at the parish church. I hadn't eaten breakfast before leaving, since I'd gotten up too late. After Mass, Mister Pawlowski, whom I didn't yet know at the time, took me along with him to a café. And since I was too embarrassed to admit I still hadn't had breakfast, I had to drink on an empty stomach, since it isn't the custom there to drink with a snack. He then took me to the meeting of the scout troop, of which he was the head. There Mister Ciemniak came looking for me, indignant that I still hadn't eaten breakfast while they were waiting for me. After the meeting, Mister Pawlowski took us to his place for breakfast, and there, for the first time in France, I ate Polish sausage. Finally Ira and Mrs. Ciemniak, unable to wait for me any longer, took the trouble to come and fetch me for breakfast themselves , but since it was already past one in the afternoon, it turned into lunch instead. After lunch, back to the tavern again, where I met some Englishmen and struck up an acquaintance with them. I beat them at billiards about forty francs and I drank beer in very great quantities, because they [the English] only drink beer and are not allowed to be sold spirits , this is strictly forbidden by the English authorities.
