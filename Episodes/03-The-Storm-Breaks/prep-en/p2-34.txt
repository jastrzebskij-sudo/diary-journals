While drinking they raise a toast with the Polish words "Na zdrowie" (To your health). We got acquainted very warmly, and they took my Polish formation commemorative badges and gave me an English rifleman's lanyard cord and a cap badge. The English army impressed me greatly with its discipline. Before evening Ira came and wanted to take me to the cinema, but because of the Englishmen I didn't go.

May sixth, nineteen forty, Monday I spend the whole day in Irenka's company, and only went with Mister Ciemniak to report to the gendarmerie. I am starting to like Ira more and more. The food is purely Polish and plentiful, and on top of that all the household members keep urging me to eat. In general, from the very first moment I feel as if I were with a very close family.

May seventh, nineteen forty, Tuesday I feel a growing fondness for Irenka, and when Mama moved away, our first kiss took place. In the evening, while at Mrs. Lutomska's, we meet two English sergeants and they invite us to a café the following evening.
