August 20, 1941, Wednesday
The shirt thieves, namely Walmus and Szymański Mieczysław, were punished by today's order with 15 days of strict arrest, of which 8 days in a cell. In the evening the gendarmes brought in legionnaires who had deserted from the Legion two days ago. They were placed under arrest right away.

August 21, 1941, Thursday
For July and August we were not issued soap, and because of this I haven't washed with soap for several days.

August 22, 1941, Friday
Nothing special.

August 23, 1941, Saturday
At 10 in the morning a medal ceremony took place for 5 people; among them is one Pole who has served 15 years in the Legion. Poles were also present during the decoration ceremony, after which a parade took place.

(Clipping pasted in from a French newspaper)
Berlin presents a new tally of the most murderous fighting in military and humanitarian history. According to the Wehrmacht, Russian losses have reached 5 million killed, yet 140 Soviet divisions remain in the line.

August 24, 1941, Sunday
The choir did not sing at Mass because Szpotnar has no time to rehearse since he plays in the orchestra. In the afternoon Poles and legionnaires played a match against the Arabs, whose goalkeeper was from Saïda's first team, and won 3–7. In the evening the garrison orchestra performed in town, and because of this roll call was at 22:00.

August 25, 1941, Monday
We received the 3rd injection and turned in our mattresses, and in exchange got straw pallets. We were also issued soap coupons, and finally there is something to wash with. On Saturday the Red Cross gave us 10 dkg of soap each.
London radio reports the entry of English and Soviet troops into Iran (Persia).

August 26, 1941, Tuesday
We saw the Arabs eating young leaves, or fruit of the cactus — I don't know what they're properly called. This fruit has a sweet taste but is dangerous, because as self-defense it has countless tiny little spines which, on contact, stay in your hand and are hard to get rid of. Many of our fellows caused themselves pain with it, but I found a way around it. Namely, I watched how the Arabs do it, and they do it in such a way that before picking the fruit they knock all the spines off it with branches. I tried it that way and it worked well.

August 27, 1941, Wednesday
We got from the Red Cross a shot in the pocket of 50 francs each and set off, four of us, to the cinema. The film was good. During

the intermission Lieutenant Raczyński told us that an order had come for departure to the Sahara, and that the first group is to be in Colomb-Béchar by September 5.

August 28, 1941, Thursday
The garrison orchestra's bandmaster has been transferred to Senegal and said goodbye to us very warmly, even though he is a German. A new sergeant has taken his place.

August 29, 1941, Friday
The orchestra performed on the barracks square, but it came out terribly badly because the new bandmaster is no good.

August 30, 1941, Saturday
By order of Major Fuzeau, a Pole, Sergeant Hankiewicz, becomes bandmaster. In Poland he was bandmaster of the 20th Infantry Regiment, and yesterday's bandmaster is now to handle only administrative matters. The orchestra is very pleased with this change.

August 31, 1941, Sunday
It is the holiday of the French combatants. It was honored with a service, then speeches and a parade. We took part in the capacity of observers. After supper a football match took place against a Saïda selection. It was won by the Poles 3–2, though it wasn't interesting. At the same time as the match, the further part of the morning's celebrations took place in town, where the garrison orchestra performed.

September 1, 1941, Monday
Today we begin the 3rd year of the war. Those who had not yet gotten the third injection received it today.

September 2, 1941, Tuesday
My name day we celebrated, the three of us, with Zarychta and Dziadkowiec, with a modest little supper.

September 3, 1941, Wednesday
Those leaving tomorrow for the Sahara are drawing their equipment today. In the evening I was at the cinema with Zarychta and Kosiński, watching the film King Kong.

September 4, 1941, Thursday
The first group, under the command of one of the officers who came from Mascara, numbering 23, left for Colomb-Béchar. Among them, of my closest friends, went Zarychta W., Zelek S., Kowacz J. I saw them off at the station, and there Lieutenant Kowalski took a farewell photograph of us.

September 5, 1941, Friday – September 6, 1941, Saturday
News reached us that Major Fuzeau has been transferred from Saïda, and everyone expresses regret about it, because he was a great friend to us.

September 7, 1941, Sunday
One of the finest matches in Saïda took place; the game was very hard-fought, and the Poles won 2–4.

September 8, 1941, Monday
In the evening, at the officers' casino, a farewell dinner was held on the occasion of Major Fuzeau's departure for Dakar to become chief of a half-brigade. During the dinner the Polish choir sang, in reduced numbers due to lack of space, along with Spaniards and the garrison orchestra.

September 9, 1941, Tuesday
Before noon Major Fuzeau said farewell to the garrison, after which a parade took place. The Poles did not take part in the farewell. At noon Major F. left by bus for Oran, and the garrison orchestra played during his departure.
News from the Soviet front is heartening, because the Russians are defending themselves bravely and for several days have even been conducting an offensive on the central front; Odessa and Leningrad are putting up fierce resistance. Let them slaughter each other as much as possible — they're both worth as much as each other.
Yesterday I got a letter from Wicek Zarychta saying that they were received warmly in Colomb-Béchar and that the matter does not look as dire as they had supposed.

September 10, 1941, Wednesday
No changes.

September 11, 1941, Thursday
Lieutenant Piątkowski gathered the choir and said that the French authorities in Oran are asking that the Polish choir give 3 concerts in that city. One for the garrison officers, another for certain sub-units, and a third for school youth. He stressed the propaganda significance of this event and asked that the choir make every effort, and that he would take care of what he could, and that those who had had their Red Cross benefits suspended would probably get them back. But there turned out to be a few individuals who, feeling that they were needed, set their own demands as a condition, and a quarrel arose over this, and how it actually ended I don't know because we dispersed for dinner, but in the order there was a release from duty for all choir members. Quite a while ago old clothes came from America through the Red Cross, but only today were they given out, and I had my jacket exchanged for a better one, though the lining is very worn and torn.
Going out on my daily evening walk I encountered a funeral that amazed me by its scale and by the fact that it was in white rather than in crepe. A white hearse, horses covered in white caparisons, and young people dressed in white. Later I learned that it was the funeral of a young unmarried woman. She was among the prettiest in Saïda and apparently died as the result of an accident with a primus stove.

September 12, 1941, Friday
The chief engaged me to make copies from ID documents for sending them to Vichy for the purpose of granting combatant badges.

September 13, 1941, Saturday
The parish priest of Saïda asked the choir to sing at High Mass tomorrow.

September 14, 1941, Sunday
At High Mass two choirs sang, Polish and French, and only here could one really notice how much better our choir sings. In the evening the garrison orchestra performed in town. Hankiewicz was a bit tipsy and conducted very well. Despite the holiday the choir rehearsed the whole afternoon. For several days now the weather has turned bad, rain falling all day with only small breaks.
Radio from London reports that Finland has ceased military operations against Russia.

September 15, 1941, Monday
Returning from an evening walk with Seidel, we heard a Polish melody ("Rozszumiały się wierzby płaczące") being sung by young French men and women, since they had heard us singing it and liked it very much.

September 16, 1941, Tuesday — The choir is preparing for Oran
September 17, 1941, Wednesday

September 18, 1941, Thursday
At the evening news there was another brawl — Kula and Szpetnar beat up Czajkowski and Łukawski, the reason for which, as everyone claims, was reporting to the Chancellery everything anyone said in the company. About Łukawski (the company chronicler) it is being said, in turn, that he was supposed to have written in the chronicle that the choir is now waiting for someone to die so it can earn something, and that we concern ourselves too much with individual matters and not general ones. I haven't read the chronicle and don't know if it's true; I only write what the general opinion says.

September 19, 1941, Friday
The choir does not rehearse today because it was assigned to work. I gathered that this is connected with yesterday's brawl, and Kula and Szpetnar were punished with 8 days of arrest.

September 20, 1941, Saturday
Right after the flag was raised, a briefing was held at which Lieutenant Piątkowski discussed our financial situation, asked that we work willingly and do at least something at the stadium, because the command had summoned him several times saying that we do nothing, and that Grandjean writes reports that the Poles sit around at the stadium all day and thereby demoralize his laborers, and he explained a few more rumors as well. Several men attacked the company sergeant-major, Benal Rudolf, saying he treats people improperly, but I saw no grounds for it. Wiktor Kosiński is treating me to the cinema because he works at the officers' casino and always earns a few francs. The film

was good. Before evening, Mr. Czapski arrived, delegate of the Committee for Aid to Poles, as the P.C.K. [Polish Red Cross] is now called.

September 21, 1941, Sunday
At High Mass our choir sang together with the French choir in alternation, as it was the previous Sunday. Mr. Czapski was present at High Mass. He was then present at dinner and supper and went around the rooms talking with individual people about their needs. After dinner a football match took place between Saïda I and Sidi Bel Abbès. Our team played beforehand against the Arabs and won for the 4th time, 3–1. Saïda also won, 2–0; Sidi Bel Abbès played very poorly. Only the goalkeeper played brilliantly. It rained the whole evening, because of which the Sunday concert was cancelled. I sent a letter to Wicek Zarychta through a lieutenant whom Mr. Czapski sent to Colomb-Béchar.

September 22, 1941, Monday
September 23, 1941, Tuesday — The choir rehearses
September 24, 1941, Wednesday
Lieutenant Piątkowski received a telephone message that tomorrow a colonel from Mascara would arrive, and he ordered a list of tank crewmen to be drawn up.

September 25, 1941, Thursday
Unexpectedly we got an order to move to the laborers' barracks. We were getting ready for the move when the colonel arrived, and the move took place with him present. We had to haul heavy iron beds and whole bundles of equipment like pots, buckets, plates, and tables with benches. It lasted until noon. The barracks here are poor but much better than in Argelès. The French colonel apparently came in connection with engaging us for the mechanical workshops in Mascara. What the outcome of this was I don't know. In the afternoon Lieutenant Zawalski arrived and brought news that apparently the camp from Morocco is on its way to Saïda. I am going to bed earlier because I am very tired, since I haven't worked this hard in a long time as I did today.

September 26, 1941, Friday
In the new camp we feel more at ease; we have a beautiful view of Saïda here, the food is more plentiful and better made. One weak point of the camp is the lack of sewerage, and because of this the latrine buckets have to be carried out far into the field twice a day. Naturally this causes a lot of trouble because no one wants to do it.

September 27, 1941, Saturday
September 28, 1941, Sunday
The choir sang at High Mass as it did last Sunday, and in the afternoon 15 Poles arrived at the camp from the camp in Morocco; they say they are the remnant of that camp, since most are in England or on their way there. They managed to slip away. These men had also tried to escape but were caught by police.

September 29, 1941, Monday
Among those who arrived yesterday is one very good tenor. My friends taught me to play poker, and I kill all my free moments with it now. A new garrison commandant arrived; he is a French captain.

September 30, 1941, Tuesday
October 1, 1941, Wednesday
Wiktor Kosiński no longer went to the casino today because our officers now dine at the hotel, and only 6 people remain at the casino, so one is enough. On the staff there is a legionnaire.

October 2, 1941, Thursday
Somehow nothing new is happening. We have more peace and quiet here than in the barracks.

October 3, 1941, Friday
Many legionnaires have joined the garrison orchestra, and because of this our men were removed from it.

October 4, 1941, Saturday
I'm having a very unlucky day at cards; playing just for 10 centimes I lost about 30 fr.

October 5, 1941, Sunday
I was at High Mass. The choir did not sing because the French were singing, and the company goes to church individually. In the afternoon a football match took place at the municipal stadium between Saïda and Oran (sailors); Saïda lost 3–1. I watched the match from the green stand because entry costs 8 fr. 50 cm., which is expensive for our pockets.

October 6, 1941, Monday
They don't exchange our sleeping bags for us the way they did with the sheets, so we have to wash them ourselves, and washing one is a nasty job and uses a lot of soap. That's just what I did today.

October 7, 1941, Tuesday
The new garrison commandant carried out an inspection of our barracks. Everyone living in them stood at assembly. The captain asked the barracks commander, Mr. G., what we do and whether we have only these one set of overalls. And he answered that he had offered us more but we didn't want to accept it. The captain reportedly said that tomorrow a colonel, a friend of the Poles, would arrive and it would somehow get sorted out. After the inspection I went for a walk. We now have a second spring. All the plants scorched by the summer sun have come back to life, and there are many flowers. I brought Wiktor a nice little bouquet from that walk. Radio reports that the Germans have abandoned Leningrad, directing their offensive at Moscow.

October 8, 1941, Wednesday
About two weeks ago Dzikowski and Klewka slipped away from the camp, and today Piekarski and Dzida did. Our authorities reported these incidents to the French authorities. Today a French general, commander of the sub-division from Tlemcen, paid us a visit. He asked about our life, work, and clothing, even though we do not belong to him. This visit raised us in Grangeon's eyes. I attend choir rehearsals, but I am singing worse and worse.

October 9, 1941, Thursday
The gendarmerie brought in Frydman, who had slipped away a few months ago. He was taken straight to arrest.

October 10, 1941, Friday — As usual
October 11, 1941, Saturday
October 12, 1941, Sunday
At Mass the choir sang, and there was a duet by Mr. Kwaśniak and Mr. Konewka.
After dinner Saïda played a match against Sidi-Bel-Abbès. It lost 2–1. Having no money, I was in the green stand, as most of the Poles do. During the match, apparently one of the Poles was flashing a mirror at the stands. The police searched Laszkiewicz for a mirror but did not find one.

October 13, 1941, Monday
Reveille is an hour earlier and we go to work at the shooting range, about 4 km away, where we are repairing the embankments. Dinner was brought to us there, and on top of that Grandjean gave us a pack of cigarettes each.
Tadek Mazur went for bread and wine to Naizarek and swore a great deal because Naizarek, which had looked so close, turned out to be far away, and he lost 3 hours of time over it.

October 14, 1941, Tuesday — Nothing special
October 15, 1941, Wednesday
In the afternoon hours a French general, commander of the Oran division, visited us. He took a lively interest in our affairs and expressed admiration for our moral bearing. The laborers, during this visit as during the previous one, were not at the assembly but were working.
Radio reports that the Germans are 100 km from Moscow and are still advancing. That Polish units are in Tobruk.

October 16, 1941, Thursday
The company went today to work at the airfield. I got out of it because my boots are badly torn.

October 17, 1941, Friday
A day off from work after yesterday's labor.

October 18, 1941, Saturday
The Vox cinema is showing the film "Taras Bulba." It is a film set against the fighting of the Cossacks with the Poles. I saw it and liked it very much.

October 19, 1941, Sunday
The singing in church went very well. In the afternoon our football team played one of the finest matches against Saïda's first team, drawing 2–2. Three of our players will now play for Saïda's team. They are Oprych, Apollo, and Tomaszewski.

October 20, 1941, Monday
Slippers came again from Algiers, but they're too small for me. Lieutenant Piątkowski told me to find some in town myself and he'd give me money to buy them.

October 21, 1941, Tuesday
I am working at the shooting range.

October 22, 1941, Wednesday
I am looking for slippers in town but cannot find any.

October 23, 1941, Thursday
Some Spaniard beat up Sierota in town for going around with Spanish girls.

October 24, 1941, Friday
The Spaniard beat up Sierota again, even though the Poles had organized a manhunt against him.

October 25, 1941, Saturday
No sandals can be found anywhere in town, so I gave up further searching.

October 26, 1941, Sunday
In the afternoon a football match took place, Saïda–Oran, ending in a 3–3 draw. Poles did not play. I was in the green stand because of lack of money. The day is overcast and very cold.

October 27, 1941, Monday
Nothing worth noting.

October 28, 1941, Tuesday
The chief announced there is demand for 30 men to work picking olives. The pay is 20 fr. per 100 kg of olives picked, and it is possible to pick 200 kg. From the earnings one has to pay 15 fr. for board, which the farmer deducts at payment. Kosiński and I signed up.

October 29, 1941, Wednesday
From dawn we work picking olives. But with the greatest effort the three of us can pick 200 kg, so it turned out we had to pay extra toward our board without even thinking about earnings. But our officers asked us to go once more tomorrow, saying they would arrange something so we wouldn't work for nothing.
