Saturday, March twenty-third, nineteen forty , Preparations for the święcone [the Easter blessing of food], and before evening the Polish święcone on the assembly square of the second Battalion. In the middle of the square, five tables were set up, decorated with the flags of the allied nations and covered with sheets in place of tablecloths. Each company brought its own eggs, cold cuts, and wine , a certain proportion of it, of course. Everything reminded us of the święcone back home.

On one side of the tables the first Battalion lined up, and on the other, the second Battalion.

All around us plenty of the local people watched this traditional observance with great curiosity, since they have nothing like it themselves.

The battalion commanders arrive, Major Zyrkiewicz and Major Harbowski; one of the captains gives the report, there is a greeting, and each major passes along the front of his Battalion.

Meanwhile the chaplain arrived, visiting us for the first time, and gave a moving address. He then blessed the tables and shared an egg with the battalion commanders and the delegates of each company.

After that the companies dispersed, taking their święcone food with them.

Sunday, March twenty-fourth, nineteen forty , Easter.

Early in the morning we go to church, passing Frenchmen sweeping the streets along the way, and even one man out on the street weaving chair seats.

The service was conducted by a Polish priest, who gave such a moving sermon that many secretly wiped tears from their eyes. In this sermon the chaplain drew a connection between the Resurrection of Christ and the Resurrection of Poland.
