May thirteenth, nineteen forty, Monday. End of leave. From morning I get ready to leave. Ira packed so much food into my suitcase that I was almost ashamed to accept it. Mister and Mrs. Ciemniak said goodbye to me very warmly, and Ira walked me to the station, about four kilometers away. I am very grateful to her for that. In farewell she kissed me very tenderly and I felt that she was very sorry that I was leaving. At the station in Danai, where I changed trains, I met several Polish soldiers and traveled with them past Reims to the central station for men on leave.

May fourteenth, nineteen forty, Tuesday I stay at this station for thirty-six hours because of continuous alarms and the track being occupied by troop transports. Finally, before evening, I depart, and by morning I am at the second central leave-station in Bicon.

May fifteenth, nineteen forty, Wednesday I am now traveling illegally, by regular passenger train, in the direction of Marseille. My travel companion is a corporal of the Czech army. A very warm relationship developed between us.

May sixteenth, nineteen forty, Thursday The train I was on didn't stop at Orange, and only stopped at Avignon. It was only half an hour later that I had a train back to Orange. I arrived at Ste Cecile before dinner, and here no changes had yet occurred. Corporal Dzierzyk has not yet returned.
