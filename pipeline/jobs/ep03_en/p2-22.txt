Sunday, February twenty-fifth, nineteen forty

After the service, at which the parish priest (a Frenchman who doesn't know Polish) intoned "Boze coś Polske," I lost six francs playing cards with Sergeant Gbur. At two in the afternoon we go on a trip into the mountains, with a French corporal named Roman and Corporal Swiatkowski Tadeusz. We walked over three hours one way. In the town of Gigondes we stopped to buy wine. When the restaurant owner learned we were Poles, he wouldn't take payment from us for the two liters of wine. On top of the rocks we built a cairn of stones with a note bearing a suitable inscription, tucked inside my old worn coin purse. Then we sang the national anthem and headed back down. Along the way we stopped once more at the little café mentioned above, and ate a supper consisting of an omelet, two liters of wine, roasted potatoes, cheese, and stewed pears. In the place there were also three officers of the French air force and six privates of the same unit. The restaurant owner's daughters, very pretty and pleasant girls, took such good care of us that I count that evening among the nicest of my life. I returned to Ste Cecile at twenty-four:thirty (half past midnight).

Monday, February twenty-sixth, nineteen forty

I am very tired from yesterday's outing, and the toe which I bruised on that outing hurts a great deal and has swollen up.
