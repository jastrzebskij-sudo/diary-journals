A poor but very pleasant little church , it could barely hold one company. On top of that a few airmen arrived who are also awaiting departure, along with a few civilians. During the service we sang church hymns, and at the end, "Boze coś Polske" [a Polish patriotic hymn]. Then the priest said a prayer in French for Poland and France. After the service the company marched back to quarters in formation of fours, singing. The French came out onto the road and watched us, because they generally don't sing. We showed them that we are soldiers, and they immediately began treating us differently , they even salute our officers now. After lunch I enjoy a swim in the sea.

July first, nineteen forty. Corporal Sadecki and I do our laundry in a nearby stream. The very clean, soft water, together with a very sunny day, helped me wash three shirts, two pairs of underpants, two towels, and one pair of socks , within three hours they were completely dry and neatly folded into squares. Corporal Sodecki was quite amazed at how cleanly they were washed. In the afternoon I take over duty as NCO of the day for the company. The company is only supposed to form up for roll call three times a day, and even that they don't want to do. I had to put in a lot of effort to get even half the company to assemble. The heat is unbearable , so much so that even sitting in the barrack without moving, everyone sweats until the sweat runs down them in streams.

July second, nineteen forty, Tuesday A company of black soldiers from Sudan arrives at the camp. Black as tar, with hair on their heads like young lambs.
