Near Avignon our officers went to get their bearings on the situation, and apparently we are to head toward Bordeaux. That is for now as regards events from the theater of war, but I had one personal incident with Sergeant Gbur which I nevertheless want to remember. While stationed at Roanne we received an order to prepare and service the tanks, because in an hour we were going into action. And the sergeant ordered my crew to bring coffee for himself and for his own crew, of course, and I refused. A row started; the sergeant wanted to put me on report for failure to carry out an order, but seeing that everyone was laughing at him, he gave up. It is in difficult moments that one truly gets to know people, and so it was again with Sergeant Gbur. Now that we are leaving France today and losing our pay, he says that we should go back to Poland, under Russian partition, because he must think of his wife and old age, and in the army he'll never live to see a pension. In Avignon we were supplied with fuel and provisions, and from Ste Cecile three Polish soldiers joined us, and we travel through Alès, Florac. In the area of Florac we pass through beautiful, high mountains. The journey is like a beautiful sightseeing trip. Then through Millau, Montauban to Toulouse. There, at the Polish consulate, they supply us with fuel and provisions and order us to head toward Foix, Perpignan, to the port of Vendres, right by the Spanish border on the Mediterranean Sea. They are directing us to such a small port because all the ports on the Atlantic are occupied by the Germans. The transport is led by Lieutenant Stanek; in the company there is great discontent over the long stops and the cooking of meals.
